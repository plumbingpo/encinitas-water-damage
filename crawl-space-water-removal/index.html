Crawl Space Water Removal in Encinitas

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

Warning Signs

  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Our Process

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Weakened Floor Framing

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.
