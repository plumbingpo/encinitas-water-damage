Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Encinitas Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

What It Looks Like

  • Drywall that sags, bows or feels soft when pressed gently
  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
  • Seams, tape lines and fastener heads becoming visible across the ceiling
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Above

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

Our Process

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Water Travels Sideways

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. We check beyond the visible stain so soaked sections down the line are not overlooked.

Wet Lights and Wiring

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

Older Ceiling Materials

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.
